What we found in four markets, tested honestly.

Thousands of rule versions across memecoins, crypto, stocks and forex. Every one was judged on data it was not chosen on: locked years, hidden markets, tests saved to git before they ran. This page sums up what failed and what held up. It doesn't say these markets can't be beaten, only that nothing we built beat them on this much data.

Memecoins on Solana

Trade Desk: a bot that scouts brand-new Solana coins, screens them with Solana Pulse safety checks, and paper-trades them with different exit rules.

LOST
No lane, exit rule or entry filter made money.Buying new memecoins from public scans loses by design: the coins that run 13×+ are the same minutes-old pools that rug.

What we found out

The money goes to insiders.Wallets that held tokens before trading opened made +1,816 SOL. Every group that bought after launch lost: about 8,900 SOL combined. By the time a bot sees a coin, it is already late.
"Smart wallets" were mostly luck.51 wallets won on 3+ early coins. On later coins they won only 39% of the time and lost 12.6 SOL in total.
Stops don't stop rugs.Crash fills came in at −70% to −100%. Three rugs caused $224 of the first run's $238 in losses.
Spotting scams works; trading doesn't.Coins Pulse rated AVOID were 82% dead soon after, and 0 of 15 SAFE coins died. Telling rugs apart is possible. Profiting from new coins is a different problem.

Crypto: established Solana tokens

Not new launches: timing rules on established tokens (SOL, JUP, BONK, WIF), plus a check of whether safety ratings match what happened next.

LOST
0 of 436 rules made money and beat simply holding in both periods.Random entry and exit times with the same amount of trading produced 2. Being out of a rising market costs more than timing saves.

What we found out

In a rising market, holding wins.Every token rose 19–35% in the test period. A rule that sits in cash part of the time has to be very good to keep up.
Mean reversion was the only family that showed signs of life.Buying sharp dips below the Bollinger band on JUP and WIF beat random timing. That is still 3 hits out of 436 tries, so it needs a forward test.
Established tokens behave nothing like new launches.All 15 coins Pulse rated SAFE (WBTC, ETH, Bonk, HYPE, ZEC and others) were alive and just moved with the market. 82% of the AVOID coins died.
Seven months is short.One trend, one market mood. The stock and forex tests below used 5–20 years for that reason.

Regular stocks (US ETFs)

Night Desk: an overnight desk that searches for stock rules and paper-trades only the ones that beat holding the S&P 500 (SPY) after costs.

LOST
No timing rule beat just holding SPY.An AI-assisted search found 3 that did beat SPY on the data they were picked from. On the locked final year they made a third of what holding made.

What we found out

Holding the index is a hard benchmark.Momentum, breakouts, pullbacks, trend filters and sector rotation either lost to SPY or only beat it on the data they were tuned on.
Trend filters trade return for comfort.They cut drawdowns in some years but cost more return than they saved.
Count your tries.With 27 rules tested, the adjusted confidence that the "winners" were real was only ~0.70, below the usual 0.95 bar. The locked year confirmed it was luck.
Two bugs made it look better than it was.A fake 88% drawdown came from one engine bug. A confidence check got yearly numbers where it needed daily ones. Both were caught and fixed.

Forex

It started with an old tip: "a 5/16 moving-average crossover with Japanese candlesticks on EUR/USD is profitable." We tested that, everything people usually add to it, and then effects from published research.

MIXED
Indicator systems: no real edge. One effect from published research held up.The best EUR/USD system lost on 9 other pairs and on 2005–2015. Selling the dollar on Fed announcement days held on 4 pairs over 21 years.

What we found out

Costs decide short timeframes.On 15-minute and 1-hour charts the spread ate every rule. One rule paid ~14,800 pips in spread over 6 years.
Stops were the ingredient that mattered.On 4h charts the move-to-breakeven and trailing stop did more than any indicator.
An effect with a reason beats a chart pattern.The only thing that held up is a dollar flow with a known cause, not an indicator mix.
Mixing unrelated systems helps.Trend + Fed day raised return per drawdown from 0.41 to 0.63, about 16%/yr at a 25% max drawdown in a typical backtest year. All 4 systems now run as a forward paper test, started Nov 9, 2026.

How we tested, and why most backtests lie

Our biggest early "wins" (+2,000% a trade, a 6,526× coin, Sharpe ratios above 3) were all bugs in the test, not edges in the market. These rules are how we caught them.

  1. Real data, named. Every price has a named source. Missing data is "unknown", never filled in.
  2. No look-ahead. A signal on a candle's close fills at the next candle's open. If a stop and a target hit in the same candle, the stop counts first.
  3. Real costs. Measured spreads per currency pair, 5 bps a side for ETFs, 1% a side and −100% write-offs for dead memecoins.
  4. Several periods, not one total. A rule has to make money in each period, not just overall.
  5. Locked data. The newest stock year was never written to disk during the search, and the script refuses a second run. Forex kept 9 other pairs and 2005–2015 hidden.
  6. Save the test before running it. Hidden-data tests were committed to git first, so nobody could adjust them after seeing results.
  7. Count the tries. Adjust confidence for how many rules were tested. For settings grids, report how much of the grid works, not the best cell.
  8. Compare with doing nothing clever. Stocks vs holding SPY, crypto vs holding the token, rules vs random timing.
  9. Then forward-test. The future is the only data none of these systems has seen.
